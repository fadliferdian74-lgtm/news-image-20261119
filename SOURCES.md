# Sources — "The World Today", Thursday 19 November 2026

Every story on the front page was reported on Thursday 19 November 2026 and
is confirmed by at least two independent credible outlets (wire services and
major media). Facts and figures below are exactly as reported; the page's
labels match them. Claims that a source itself flags as one side's account
are framed as claims on the page. Items that could not be confirmed by two
independent outlets, or whose events belonged to another day, were dropped
(see the exclusion list at the end).

## Lead story

1. **Trump, Xi and Putin hold first three-way summit**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 19); Wall Street Journal (Nov 19); also CNN,
     Le Monde and The Times live coverage.
   - Facts: The US, Chinese and Russian presidents met on Thursday at
     Kylin Villa in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the APEC summit — the
     first three-way leaders' meeting of the three countries. The talks
     ran about 90 minutes (WSJ), after a roughly 40-minute Putin–Trump
     bilateral. The joint statement's four points: reform the United
     Nations; increase production and supply of refined petroleum
     products and keep energy flows stable; state consent and officials'
     immunity are paramount and the ICC "must not abuse jurisdiction";
     and "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought", with
     dialogue on nuclear risk reduction and non-proliferation. Trump
     told reporters "We are really making history" and rated the meeting
     "a 12" out of 10 to NBC.

## Main stories

2. **Anthropic closes up 17% in record IPO debut**
   - Sources: Investopedia (Nov 19); Wall Street Journal (Nov 19); also
     Fast Company, Barron's and the Motley Fool.
   - Facts: Anthropic priced its IPO on Wednesday at $83 a share, above
     the $70–78 range, raising a record $81.3bn and valuing the company
     near $1.5 trillion. The shares (ticker ANT, Nasdaq) opened Thursday
     just before noon at $111.50, reached $115.10 and closed at $97.17,
     up 17.07%, a value near $1.76 trillion. Roughly 813–840 million
     shares changed hands, beating SpaceX's opening-day volume record.

3. **Houthi strike hits Riyadh's tallest tower**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 19); AFP (Nov 19); also the Wall Street
     Journal.
   - Facts: A Houthi attack at dawn on Thursday damaged the PIF Tower in
     Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District — at 80 storeys the Saudi
     capital's tallest building and the Public Investment Fund's HQ.
     Windows were blown out across seven or eight floors and the facade
     was bent; there were no injuries. The district was closed and
     evacuated. The Saudi-led coalition spokesman said the damage came
     from debris of an intercepted missile; the Houthis claimed a second
     attack in the evening. It was the second financial-district landmark
     hit in under two weeks, after the Aramco building on Nov 8.

4. **COP31 draft drops all fossil-fuel language**
   - Sources: Reuters (Nov 19); IPS / Global Issues (Nov 19).
   - Facts: A revised COP31 draft text published in Antalya on Thursday
     removed every reference to fossil fuels and dropped the proposed
     $120bn-a-year adaptation-finance target, drawing fierce backlash
     from vulnerable countries and civil-society groups. The draft is
     the first COP text to acknowledge that warming will exceed 1.5°C.
     It keeps a goal of electricity reaching 35% of final energy by
     2035 and launches the Antalya Compact for Energy Efficient
     Buildings and an Antalya-to-Addis Ababa adaptation-finance roadmap.
     The talks end on Friday Nov 20.

5. **GTA VI launches to near-perfect reviews**
   - Sources: Le Monde (Nov 19); CNN (Nov 19); also Rockstar / Take-Two.
   - Facts: Grand Theft Auto VI was released on Thursday Nov 19 at
     midnight local time for PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S, priced
     $79.99, with no PC version. Xbox users played early via the
     New Zealand region trick. It holds a Metacritic score of 99 from
     150 reviews (all positive) and an OpenCritic score of 99, and took
     more than $1.5bn in pre-sales (CNN).

## Briefs

6. **Senate rejects Iran war-powers resolution** — Reuters; USA Today.
   The Senate voted 48–51 on Thursday against a resolution requiring
   congressional approval for strikes on Iran. The House had passed its
   version (H.Con.Res.93) in September, 220–204. Collins, Murkowski and
   Paul voted yes; Fetterman was the lone Democratic no.

7. **Projectile lands behind Dutch embassy in Kyiv** — Reuters; NRC.
   The Netherlands summoned Russia's ambassador on Thursday after a
   projectile landed behind the Dutch embassy in Kyiv on Thursday
   afternoon, causing minor damage and no injuries (Foreign Minister
   Tom Berendsen).

8. **Somalia expels UAE ambassador in sovereignty row** — Reuters; AP.
   Somalia on Thursday ordered the UAE ambassador to leave within 72
   hours and recalled its own envoy, citing sovereignty violations its
   information minister called "an open invasion". The UAE replied that
   it had already closed its Mogadishu embassy in January 2026.

9. **Parliament accepts president's resignation (Mongolia)** — AP;
   Montsame. Parliament accepted President Khurelsukh Ukhnaa's
   resignation on Thursday by 96 votes of 104 present. He had announced
   it on Nov 17 after a party rival alleged a $25m railway bribery
   scheme, which he denies. Speaker Sandagiin Byambatsogt acts as
   president; an election follows within four months.

10. **Student List lecturer becomes acting president (Serbia)** — AP;
    CORD Magazine. Aleksandar Cupic of the Student List was elected
    parliament speaker on Thursday with 140 votes to 97 and becomes
    acting president after President Vucic's September resignation. The
    Student List's Oct 25 election win ended the SNS's 14-year rule; a
    presidential election is due by Dec 27.

11. **Odisha shelters 300,000 ahead of Cyclone Akvan** — Reuters;
    The Hindu BusinessLine. By Thursday evening Odisha had moved
    2,98,676 people into 2,523 relief camps (printed on the page in
    international grouping as 298,676), with 423 rescue teams deployed
    and 9,341 shelters ready. Akvan is India's third cyclone in a
    month; landfall was expected Friday near Gopalpur.

12. **Top court overturns ex-PM Han's 15-year sentence** — Reuters;
    Korea JoongAng Daily. South Korea's Supreme Court on Thursday
    overturned in part former prime minister Han Duck-soo's 15-year
    prison sentence over the 2024 martial-law episode, sent the case
    back to the Seoul High Court and extended his detention by two
    months.

13. **Ex-first lady's four-year jail term upheld** — Reuters; Korea
    JoongAng Daily. The same court on Thursday upheld former first lady
    Kim Keon Hee's four-year prison term for stock manipulation,
    bribery and Unification Church links.

14. **Denmark ratifies US defence pact by 109-0** — AP; Reuters. The
    Folketing ratified the US–Denmark–Greenland security deal 109–0 on
    Thursday: expansion of Pituffik Space Base and new US defence areas
    at Narsarsuaq and Mestersvig, with Danish sovereignty reaffirmed.
    Greenland's Inatsisartut approved it on Nov 3; it was signed on
    Sept 22 in New York.

15. **Tribunal: Apple was ordered to open encryption** — Reuters;
    Computer Weekly. The UK's Investigatory Powers Tribunal confirmed
    on Thursday that the Home Office had served Apple a technical
    capability notice demanding access to encrypted customer data,
    rejecting the government's secrecy argument. A second, UK-only
    notice followed the first being dropped after US intervention; the
    substance hearing is expected in 2027.

16. **Japan and India deepen defence ties in Tokyo** — AP; Bharat
    Shakti. At Thursday's 2+2 meeting the two governments signed a
    letter of intent for Japanese Self-Defence Forces to hold live-fire
    training at Indian ranges for the first time, including
    surface-to-ship missiles, and advanced the UNICORN naval antenna
    transfer ("design with Japan, make in India").

17. **US sanctions scam-centre operators** — Reuters; US Treasury /
    OFAC release. Treasury sanctioned three people and two companies
    over Southeast Asia scam centres on Thursday: Cambodian operator
    Zhao Hui (whose compounds were leased from Prince Group and paid
    protection fees to Chen Zhi), his two firms, and DKBA commanders
    Gen. Saw Shwe Wah and Col. Saw A One in Myanmar.

18. **Dozens missing after migrant boat capsizes (Senegal)** — Reuters;
    AFP. A boat carrying migrants capsized off Yenne at about 02:00 on
    Wednesday; the search continued through Thursday, four bodies were
    recovered, and survivors put 50–100 people aboard. Senegal's navy
    rescued more than 1,400 people in 48 hours amid a surge toward the
    Canary Islands.

19. **Israel announces tank raid past Yellow Line (Gaza)** — Reuters;
    AFP. Israel announced on Thursday that it had concluded a tank raid
    across Gaza's Yellow Line near Nuseirat to dismantle a Hamas
    tunnel — the first publicly announced raid deep past the line since
    the October 2025 ceasefire. Families returned on Thursday and the
    Salahudeen road reopened; Gaza health officials said two
    Palestinians were killed by tank shelling in Zeitoun later Thursday.

20. **Putin drops energy truce after bridge strikes** — Reuters;
    New York Post. Speaking in Shenzhen on Thursday after Ukraine's
    third strike on the Crimea Bridge this week (early Thursday),
    President Putin said Moscow would no longer consider a moratorium
    on Black Sea or energy strikes and vowed a response "far more
    painful" than Kyiv's attack. The bridge's road section was
    seriously damaged; a ferry service was to start Friday.

21. **Trump: US and China will work on AI rules** — Reuters; Fox News.
    In a Fox News interview on Thursday, President Trump said he and
    President Xi both want AI regulations for safety purposes and will
    work together on reasonable AI rules.

22. **US official floated ceding Essequibo land (Guyana)** — Reuters;
    CaribDirect. Reuters reported on Thursday that State Department
    policy chief Jeremy Lewin had suggested in recent months that
    Guyana consider ceding part of the Essequibo to Venezuela, shocking
    Guyanese officials. The US Embassy in Georgetown and Guyana's
    foreign ministry both said on Thursday that the US position on the
    boundary has not changed.

23. **Leaders adopt the Shenzhen Outlook (APEC)** — Xinhua; AnewZ.
    APEC leaders adopted the 2026 Leaders' Declaration, the "Shenzhen
    Outlook", by consensus on Thursday, along with outcome documents on
    trade, connectivity and innovation. Xi announced $20m for APEC
    practical cooperation and a Center for Asia-Pacific Community
    Studies in Shenzhen. (Both wire reports originate from Chinese and
    partner state media; the adoption itself was also carried in the
    summit's international live coverage.)

24. **Drone strike hits Badme as border clashes grow** — ACLED; AFP.
    ACLED's Nov 19 alert recorded an Ethiopian (ENDF) drone strike on
    the border town of Badme early on Thursday that forced residents to
    flee; a Wednesday-evening strike near Biyara injured four civilians
    (BBC Amharic). ACLED warns the risk of a full-scale
    Ethiopia–Eritrea confrontation is "now high".

25. **Berlin summit urges winter energy ceasefire** — UNN (German
    government joint statement); The Times. Germany, France, Britain
    and Ukraine, meeting in Berlin on Thursday as the Coalition of the
    Willing, jointly called on Russia to accept a winter energy
    ceasefire and a Black Sea food-export truce; the EU's €90bn Ukraine
    Support Loan was pledged.
